Project summary

We trained a Unitree Go2 to find a cube, move behind it, and push it into a goal. A high-level policy decides how the robot should move; a separate locomotion policy turns that command into stable locomotion. The real system connects both policies to camera-based tracking, state estimation with ROS 2.

Why a Hierarchy?

Pushing an object looks requires stable locomotion and manipulation at the same time. The robot must keep its balance, maintain the cube in view, approach it from a useful direction, and still make progress toward a goal.

Those responsibilities are separated: the locomotion policy delivers stable locomotion at high frequency, while the pushing policy can work at a slower rate and command the velocities with input of cube and goal position.

03

Two Stage Training

Both policies were trained with PPO in Isaac Lab. First a general velocity-tracking policy was trained and then this exported policy was embedded inside the pushing environment. During the second stage, the high-level policy learns to use the locomotion controller to solve the pushing task.

Stage 1

Velocity tracking

The Go2 follows forward, lateral, and yaw commands over varied terrain. Rewards favor velocity tracking and stable, energy-conscious gait behavior while penalizing falls, sliding, joint-limit violations, and abrupt actions.

Stage 2

Cube pushing

The policy learns to approach the cube, align behind it, push toward the goal, and stop with the cube settled inside. The frozen locomotion model executes every velocity command inside the environment.

Curriculum

Command limits and reward emphasis are introduced gradually. Early training gives more help for approaching and aligning; later training puts more weight on moving the cube efficiently and completing the task.

Sim-to-real preparation

Robot and cube mass, friction, motor gains, cube scale, spawn geometry, and floor properties are randomized. Cube observations also receive delay, Gaussian noise, dropout, and occasional spikes.

The interface between the two policies is intentionally small. The high-level network produces a desired body velocity; the low-level network receives that command alongside fast proprioceptive feedback. The latest high-level command is held between its 10 Hz updates while locomotion continues at 50 Hz.

04

Cube Tracking

A single vision method was not reliable enough for the control loop. YOLOE gives a strong semantic detection but is comparatively slow. Raw point-cloud tracking can update more frequently, but sometimes tracks wrong objects.

  1. 1

    10 Hz

    Segment

    A small YOLOE segmentation model searches for the open-vocabulary class box in a 640 × 360 image.

  2. 2

    RGB-D

    Convert to 3D

    Mask pixels are mapped back to aligned depth, sampled, deprojected, and filtered with the median absolute deviation.

  3. 3

    30 Hz

    Track in PCL

    The cloud is voxelized, the floor and front-leg points are removed, and plausible cube clusters are associated with the predicted state.

  4. 4

    EKF

    Fuse

    Timestamped YOLO and PCL positions update a planar position-velocity filter with friction-aware prediction and delayed-measurement accounting.

Why delayed measurement accounting?

Segmentation results arrive later than their camera exposure. The tracker keeps a short state history, inserts the measurement at its original timestamp, and runs the following updates again. This avoids treating an old observation as if it were current.

05

From Sim to Real

State estimation

Invariant EKF odometry

For robot pose and velocity, we adapted INRIA Go2 odometry stack and its Invariant EKF. The filter propagates IMU data and corrects it with leg kinematics and foot sensor information. We moved the ROS wrapper to C++ so it runs reliably at 500 Hz.

Policy runtime

ROS 2 and TensorRT

The policies are exported to TensorRT engine files and run in separate C++ nodes. The low-level node also includes the stand-up sequence, command timeout, torque limiting, and publication of Unitree motor commands.

Y

Set the goal

The goal position is set to the current robot position.

B

Recover the cube

If tracking is lost, commands ramp downa and recovery rotates toward the last known bearing until YOLO sees the cube again. This can also be set to automatic.

✓

Finish safely

The policy stops after the cube remains inside the target for the hold time and the robot has moved clear of the goal area.
