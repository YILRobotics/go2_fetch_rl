Curriculum

Command limits and reward emphasis are introduced gradually. Early training gives more help for approaching and aligning; later training puts more weight on moving the cube efficiently and completing the task.

Sim-to-real preparation

Robot and cube mass, friction, motor gains, cube scale, spawn geometry, and floor properties are randomized. Cube observations also receive delay, Gaussian noise, dropout, and occasional spikes.

The high-level policy sends body-velocity commands at 10 Hz; the low-level policy converts them into joint targets at 50 Hz.

03

Cube Tracking

A single vision method was not reliable enough for the control loop. YOLOE gives a strong semantic detection but is comparatively slow. Raw point-cloud tracking can update more frequently, but sometimes tracks wrong objects.

  1. 1

    10 Hz

    Segment

    A small YOLOE segmentation model searches for the open-vocabulary class box in a 640 × 360 image.

  2. 2

    RGB-D

    Convert to 3D

    Mask pixels are mapped back to aligned depth, sampled, deprojected, and filtered with the median absolute deviation.

  3. 3

    30 Hz

    Track in PCL

    The cloud is voxelized, the floor and front-leg points are removed, and plausible cube clusters are associated with the predicted state.

  4. 4

    EKF

    Fuse

    Timestamped YOLO and PCL positions update a planar position-velocity filter with friction-aware prediction and delayed-measurement accounting.

Why delayed measurement accounting?

Segmentation results arrive later than their camera exposure. The tracker keeps a short state history, inserts the measurement at its original timestamp, and runs the following updates again. This avoids treating an old observation as if it were current.

04

From Sim to Real

State estimation

Invariant EKF odometry

For robot pose and velocity, we adapted INRIA Go2 odometry stack and its Invariant EKF. The filter propagates IMU data and corrects it with leg kinematics and foot sensor information. We moved the ROS wrapper to C++ so it runs reliably at 500 Hz.

Policy runtime

ROS 2 and TensorRT

Policies run as separate C++ ROS 2 nodes using TensorRT. The low-level node handles standing, timeouts, torque limits, and motor commands. For testing, the high-level policy can also use Unitree's built-in controller.

Operator controls

Remote control

The complete deployment can be operated from the handheld controller.

  • StartStand upMoves the robot from its damping pose into the standing position.
  • AEnable low-level policyRuns the locomotion policy with velocity commands from the joysticks or the high-level policy.
  • XStart or pause high-level policyToggles autonomous cube pushing.
  • YSet the goalUses the robot's current position as the goal.
  • SelectEmergency stopStops control immediately and returns the robot to its damping position.

Automatic finish: The policy stops after the cube remains inside the target and the robot has moved clear.

Future work: Extend the system to new object shapes and more dynamic tasks.
